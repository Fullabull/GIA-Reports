UNN Architecture: Own the Spine, Rent the Limbs

Version 2 - plain language, with real sizing and a draft rule set for Kim to correct

What this document is for

This is the foundation document. It describes what UNN builds and owns.

The earlier comparisons - Kajabi versus GoHighLevel, and then Cloudflare versus Mux versus Amazon versus Bunny - were both asking "which company should we hire?" That is a real question, but it is the second question. It cannot be answered well until the first one is settled.

The first question is: what does UNN own, and what does UNN rent?

Once that line is drawn, choosing a video company becomes ordinary shopping. Anyone can do it in an afternoon, and if the choice turns out badly it can be changed without disturbing anything else.

The one idea

Think of UNN as a body.

The spine is the part that must never be outsourced: who the members are, what each one is allowed to see, who has paid, and what content exists. These facts define UNN as a business. If UNN loses control of them, UNN is not really running itself.

The limbs are the specialist services that do heavy lifting: delivering video, sending email, storing files. These are useful, competitive, and interchangeable. There is no advantage in owning them, and no shame in renting them.

A simple test for whether something belongs in the spine:

If every company UNN pays disappeared overnight, what would UNN need in order to rebuild in a week instead of a year?

That is the spine. Everything else is a supplier.

How it actually works: the wristband

This is the mechanism that makes everything else possible. It is worth understanding even if nothing else here is.

Picture a venue. UNN keeps the guest list and decides who gets in. The door staff do not keep the guest list - they just check wristbands. When someone qualifies, UNN hands them a wristband that is good for one room, for a short time.

That is exactly how this works:

1. A member clicks a video on UNN's own website. 2. The website asks UNN's own system: "Is this person allowed to watch this, right now?" 3. UNN's system checks its own records and its own rules. 4. If NO - the website shows the reason: "subscribe to watch", "available to free members in 4 days", "your payment failed" 5. If YES - UNN issues a wristband: a short-lived pass that unlocks exactly that one video. 6. The video company checks the wristband is genuine and starts playing.

Notice what the video company never learns. It does not know what a "premium UNN member" is. It does not know Kim's rules. It does not know why this person qualified. It checks a wristband and plays a file. That is all it is for, and that is why it can be replaced.

"Premium" is not a sticker on a person

This is the single most important design decision, and it is easy to get backwards.

The way that traps you

Put a label on each member - PREMIUM - and store it inside the video or membership company's system. Then teach that company which labels open which doors.

Now the meaning of "premium" lives in their product. Changing what premium means is a support ticket, a migration, or a pile of automation boxes nobody can read six months later.

The way that frees you

Store the facts - joined on this date, paying, trial ends here, was given a founding-member pass. Then answer the question fresh every single time it is asked.

The meaning of "premium" lives in one place UNN controls, written in plain rules UNN can read and change.

Put simply: "premium" is not a sticker stuck on a person. It is the answer to a question UNN asks fresh, every time. The answer can depend on anything Kim wants it to depend on - and changing it means changing one rule, not changing platforms.

This is the direct answer to "we expect the membership model to keep evolving." It will. That is fine. Evolving it should cost an afternoon, not a migration.

UNN already owns the hardest part

This deserves saying plainly, because it has been missing from the discussion.

UNN already runs a working publishing system. Kim's broadcasts are captured, transcribed, edited, published, and archived - with the master files and the descriptive records in UNN's own hands, on UNN's own infrastructure.

That is the content catalogue. It is the piece most organisations at this stage still have to build, and it is normally the messiest one. UNN has it.

The existing pipeline does not get replaced by any of this. It becomes the source - it already knows what exists, when it was published, and where the master is kept. The new system reads from it.

Practical consequence: UNN is starting this project from further along than either vendor comparison assumed.

The seven things UNN writes down

The spine is smaller than it sounds. In practice it is seven lists. Everything above is built out of these.

The listWhat is in it, in plain terms
MembersA permanent UNN membership number that never changes, plus name, email, and join date. The number is UNN's own - not borrowed from any supplier.
Billing linksWhich payment company handles this member, and that company's reference numbers for them. This is the thread back to the money.
SubscriptionsWhat they are on, whether it is active, trialling, failing, or cancelled, and the important dates.
ContentEvery report and video: UNN's own ID, title, publication date, who it is for, and where the master file lives.
Delivery copiesWhich video company currently holds a playable copy of each item, and their reference for it. This one line is what makes switching suppliers possible.
Special passesAccess granted by hand, outside billing: founding members, comps, guests, event passes - each with a reason and an optional expiry.
Event historyAn add-only diary of what happened: joined, paid, payment failed, cancelled, granted access, published. Never edited, only added to.

Why special passes get their own list: so that nobody ever has to invent a fake membership tier to handle an exception. When Kim wants to give someone permanent access, that is one entry with a reason attached - not a permanent distortion of the billing records.

Why event history matters: it is the answer to "can we prove we could leave?" There is no export to demand from anybody. UNN already has the record.

The front door: knowing who someone is

Before UNN can ask "what is this person allowed to see," it has to know who they are. That is the login, and it belongs in the spine - it is the gate everything else sits behind.

  • Keep it simple and self-contained. An email address and either a password or a one-time link sent to that address. No third party required.
  • Do not make "Sign in with Google / Facebook / Apple" the only way in. Offering it is fine. Depending on it is not - it hands another company the ability to lock a UNN member out of UNN.
  • The membership number is issued once and never changes, no matter what else changes around it. Email addresses change. Payment companies change. Video suppliers change. The membership number does not.

Getting paid - and the risk nobody plans for

Most exit planning asks: what if we want to leave? The sharper question for UNN is: what if the supplier decides we have to leave?

Payment processors close accounts. They do it over content categories, sometimes with little notice, and sometimes while holding funds for an extended period. This has happened to independent media organisations. For UNN this is not a theoretical risk to file away - it is a live one, and it should be handled deliberately rather than discovered.

What to actually do

  1. Ask in writing, before committing. Send the processor a plain description of UNN's content and ask whether it is permitted under their acceptable-use terms. Get the answer in writing. Do the same for the video supplier, the email supplier, and the hosting supplier.
  2. Know the backup before it is needed. Have a second processor identified and understood, so that a termination is a bad week rather than a crisis.
  3. Keep the money reference numbers in UNN's own records. That is the "billing links" list above. It is what lets UNN reconnect members to a new processor.

A recommendation, rather than a shrug: start with Stripe. It has the best tools for this kind of system, and it stores nothing that UNN cannot reproduce. But get the written answer first, and have the alternative named.

What UNN does not need is extra machinery for this. Because access is a question UNN answers - not a label a supplier stores - the payment company is already replaceable. UNN's rules ask "is this member paid up?" They never ask "and which company told us so?" That independence is already built in; it does not need to be engineered a second time.

One boundary worth stating: the payment company is the authority on money - whether a charge succeeded. UNN is the authority on membership - what a failed charge should mean. A failed payment is a financial fact. A seven-day grace period is a UNN decision.

Kim's rules - a draft to be corrected, not a form to be filled in

This is the most valuable page in the document, and it is deliberately presented as a draft with real answers in it rather than a list of blanks.

Every answer below is a guess. They are here so that Kim can cross them out and write the real ones, which takes five minutes. A blank form takes an hour and usually does not come back.

QuestionDraft answer - please correct
What does a visitor see without signing up at all?The older archive - reports past their premium window - plus one or two current pieces chosen as an introduction to UNN.
What does a free member get after signing up, with no card?Everything a visitor sees, plus the community, plus every new report 7 days after it is published.
Is a card required to try the paid tier?No. New members get 30 days of full access with no card and no automatic charge.
What happens when the 30 days end?They quietly become a free member. Nothing is charged, nothing is deleted, they keep their community account.
What does a paying member get?New reports the moment they publish, live Q&A access, and the complete back catalogue.
What happens when a payment fails?Full access continues for 7 days while the card is retried. If it still fails, they become a free member.
Can staff grant access outside of billing?Yes - founding members, complimentary access, guests, event passes. Each one recorded with a reason and an optional expiry date.
What happens when someone cancels?They keep paid access until the end of the period they already paid for, then become a free member. Their account and history remain.
Is there a supporter tier between free and paid?To be decided - this one genuinely needs Kim's answer rather than a guess.

Each corrected answer becomes a test. Before anything is built, every rule on this list is checked against the design. If all of them can be expressed without changing suppliers, the foundation is right. If one of them cannot, that has been discovered on paper - for free - instead of six months into a build.

What it takes to build

The previous draft of this document declined to estimate. That was a mistake, and it is corrected here: a plan with no size in it cannot be approved, scheduled, or funded. These are estimates, not quotes. They are meant to be corrected by whoever builds it - not deleted.

One experienced developer. A person signs up, pays, and watches a members-only video that a non-member cannot reach. Rough edges everywhere - but it either works or it does not, and UNN finds out early.

2-4 months

After the proof. Password recovery, refunds and cancellations, staff tools, monitoring, backups, privacy and deletion handling, and the support processes around all of it.

Ongoing

Someone owns it

This is a system UNN runs, not a subscription UNN pays. That is the trade being made, and it should be made with open eyes.

Note that steps 1 through 3 involve no code and no spending, and they are where most of the risk actually lives.

Community is the hard one, and it is not solved here

Video turns out to be easy to stay independent of, because a video is just a file and a wristband. Community is not.

Community means posts, comments, private messages, groups, moderation history, who follows whom, notifications, attachments, reports and blocks. It is tangled, it is created by members rather than by UNN, and it is the one thing that suppliers genuinely do not hand back in usable form.

So this is a real decision with real consequences either way:

  • Rent it and accept that it becomes UNN's largest remaining dependency - in which case, copy the content out continuously from day one, rather than asking for an export on the way out of the door.
  • Build it and accept that it is a substantial product in its own right, considerably larger than the spine.

This deserves its own document and its own decision. What it does not deserve is to be quietly treated as one more interchangeable supplier, because it is the one place where that assumption is false.

Where the streaming comparison belongs

Cloudflare Stream, Mux, Amazon IVS, Bunny and the rest are all still relevant, and the cost work already done on them is still useful. Their place is as an appendix to this document rather than a foundation underneath it.

Switching video suppliers later is still genuine work - re-uploading files, new reference numbers, re-testing playback on phones and desktops. The point of this architecture is not that the work disappears. It is that the work stays in one room of the house: nobody has to touch the member list, the rules, the payments, or the website.

Own the spine. Rent the limbs. Decide community on purpose.
