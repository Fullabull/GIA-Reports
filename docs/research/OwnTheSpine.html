UNN Architecture: Own the Spine, Rent the Limbs

A vendor-independent starting point for a custom UNN front end and back end

Important starting assumption

This is a target architecture, not a claim about UNN's current architecture.

We do not yet know which of these capabilities UNN already has, which are partially built, or which are missing. The purpose of this page is to identify what should remain under UNN control and provide a framework for comparing that target with what already exists.

The architectural premise

The spine is the set of facts and rules that should remain true about UNN no matter which vendors UNN uses.

If every vendor account disappeared tomorrow, what would UNN need in order to rebuild without losing its members, business rules, subscription relationships, content identity, and control?

Those durable facts belong in the UNN-owned spine. Video delivery, email delivery, storage, CDN, search, and other specialized services can then be rented as replaceable limbs.

The durable UNN spine

1. Identity

Who is this person?

UNN should have its own durable member ID that does not change when a vendor changes.

2. Authentication

How does this person prove who they are?

Login, session/token issuance, password reset, verification, MFA if desired, and account recovery must be defined deliberately.

3. Billing relationship

What financial relationship belongs to this member?

UNN should preserve the billing provider and the provider-side identifiers needed to reconnect the member to billing records.

4. Membership state

What is this member's current relationship with UNN?

Free, trialing, paid, past due, canceled, comped, founding member, suspended, etc.

5. Entitlement policy

What is this member allowed to do right now, and why?

This decision point belongs to UNN, not to a video vendor, CRM, or payment processor.

6. Content catalog

What content exists?

UNN owns the content ID, title, publication date, access class, master location, and delivery-provider mappings.

7. Audit / business events

What important state changes happened?

Joined, paid, failed payment, canceled, granted access, revoked access, published, suspended, restored.

8. Administrative control

How do authorized staff inspect and correct the system?

Support and administration are part of the product, not an afterthought.

Centralize entitlement behind one UNN-owned decision point

The rest of the system should not independently decide whether a member may watch, post, download, join, or participate.

can_access(member, resource, action, now) -> { allowed, reason }

UNN evaluates its stored facts and business rules and returns the answer.

membership statustrial datesbilling statecontent publish datespecial grantsgrace periodssuspensions

This is the payoff: Kim can change the business rule without requiring the video vendor, payment provider, or front end to define what the new rule means.

Candidate business rules - intended for Kim to correct

These are not claimed requirements. They are deliberately concrete starting points based on ideas already discussed, because it is easier to correct a draft than fill in a blank page.

QuestionCandidate starting rule
What does a brand-new member get?Full access for 30 days, without requiring payment information.
What happens after the trial?If the member does not subscribe, they fall back to limited/free access rather than losing the account entirely.
When do non-paying members see new Kim content?Premium members immediately; free members after a delay such as 7 days.
Can some content be public?Yes. Individual recruitment, announcement, or public-interest items can override normal gating.
What happens after a failed payment?Keep premium access during a defined grace period, then fall back according to UNN policy.
Can staff grant access independent of billing?Yes. Temporary, complimentary, founding-member, staff, or event-specific access should be possible without inventing fake subscriptions.
What remains after cancellation?The UNN account remains; paid entitlements end according to the paid-through date, and any free/basic entitlements remain available.

Every correction becomes a test case for the entitlement engine.

Billing-provider independence - and billing-provider risk

Do not assume Stripe or any other processor until UNN's current billing setup is known.

The processor should be treated as authoritative for financial facts such as payment success/failure, subscription state, billing period, amount, refunds, and chargebacks where relevant.

UNN should preserve enough information to reconnect those facts to its own member record:

billing_provider provider_customer_id provider_subscription_id

The billing provider owns payment truth. UNN owns membership truth.
A failed payment is a financial fact. Whether that means immediate downgrade, a grace period, limited free access, or something else is a UNN business rule.

The payment processor is also a termination-risk dependency

This is not only about future flexibility. Billing is a critical external dependency.

  • Confirm that UNN's business model and content are permitted under the processor's terms.
  • Seek written confirmation where practical.
  • Understand what happens to active subscriptions if the relationship ends.
  • Verify customer/subscription export and migration options.
  • Understand whether funds can be held or processing suspended, and under what conditions.
  • Know the fallback path before an emergency occurs.

The correct processor recommendation depends on UNN's actual legal entity, geography, currencies, payment methods, subscription volume, current processor, and risk profile. Until those facts are known, naming a processor would be guessing.

The request flow: where "headless" becomes real

1. Viewer signs in through UNN's authentication layer. 2. Authentication establishes a session/token mapped to a durable UNN member ID. 3. Viewer clicks content on UNN's own front end. 4. Front end asks UNN's API for access to that content. 5. UNN loads the member, membership/billing state, and content record. 6. UNN evaluates its entitlement policy. 7. If denied, UNN returns the reason: upgrade / wait / past_due / suspended. 8. If allowed, UNN mints the current provider's short-lived playback credential. 9. The video provider streams bytes. It does not decide what "Premium UNN Member" means.

The provider adapter is the swap boundary. Changing video vendors still requires asset and integration migration, but it should not require redesigning UNN's member model or entitlement rules.

Why special entitlement grants deserve their own concept

UNN should not have to invent fake membership tiers every time Kim wants an exception.

  • founding-member access
  • complimentary access
  • temporary event access
  • extended grace periods
  • staff/moderator privileges
  • manual support corrections

Authentication deserves the same independence analysis

Authentication answers: "Prove you are this person." Identity answers: "This person is UNN member X."

UNN may build authentication itself or rent it from an identity provider. Either way, the durable UNN member identity should not become inseparable from a vendor-specific user ID.

  • account export/migration
  • session/token architecture
  • password/account recovery
  • MFA and email verification
  • vendor termination risk
  • replacement path without losing member identity

Community is the genuinely hard limb

Video delivery is relatively easy to abstract. Community is not.

  • posts and comments
  • direct messages
  • groups/channels
  • moderation history
  • member relationships
  • notifications
  • attachments
  • search indexes
  • blocks/reports/abuse state

If UNN rents this layer, it may become the largest remaining source of lock-in. If UNN builds it, it becomes a substantial software product in its own right.

Community deserves its own architectural decision.

Vendor termination risk belongs in the foundation

Exit planning usually asks: "What if UNN wants to leave?"

A more severe question is: "What if the vendor decides UNN has to leave?"

  • Is the intended use permitted?
  • What data/assets can be exported?
  • How much notice may termination provide?
  • Can service be suspended before export is complete?
  • Is an alternate provider or recovery path ready?
  • What must be independently backed up?

Apply the same test to billing, authentication, email, hosting, storage, DNS/CDN, streaming, app stores, and any rented community layer.

Own the rules; rent the machinery

Each rented service should receive only the information it needs to do its job. The service should not become the place where UNN's business meaning lives.

Administrative control is part of the real product

  • find a member
  • see why access was allowed or denied
  • grant/revoke temporary access
  • inspect billing/membership state
  • correct support issues safely
  • suspend or restore accounts
  • view important audit history

UNN cannot be operated by asking a developer to edit database rows every time a member has a problem.

How large is the core idea?

The conceptual core is small enough that a competent developer should be able to prove it with a working vertical slice in weeks, not quarters:

authenticate one test member | load membership state | evaluate one or more real UNN access rules | mint a signed playback credential | play protected content through UNN's own front end

That prototype would prove the architecture. It would not mean the production UNN platform is finished.

Production hardening still includes migration, community, administration, authentication recovery, webhook reliability, retries/idempotency, refunds/chargebacks, staff permissions, monitoring, backups, security, privacy/deletion requirements, disaster recovery, and operational support.

Magnitude, not false precision: the core decision can be tested relatively quickly; production scope must be sized against UNN's actual existing systems, migration requirements, staffing, and operating model.

Existing independent content workflows are an encouraging pattern

A content-ingestion/publishing pipeline can remain independent of the delivery platform. Source content and metadata can be produced and preserved first, then published to one or more downstream delivery systems.

The existing GIA archive workflow is a small real-world example of that separation: masters and metadata are maintained independently from the downstream web/audio delivery mechanisms.

This is an architectural example, not a claim about UNN's current production pipeline.

Where streaming vendors belong

Cloudflare Stream, Mux, Amazon IVS, Bunny/Nimble and other delivery options remain important. They are simply a downstream infrastructure decision once the UNN-owned spine is defined.

The streaming comparison is therefore best treated as an implementation-options appendix rather than the architectural foundation.
