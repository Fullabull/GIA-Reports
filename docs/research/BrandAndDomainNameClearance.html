Brand and Domain Name Clearance

Standard procedure for checking availability, legal compliance, and search viability when selecting a new network name.

5-Step Vetting Checklist

  1. 1. Broad Domain Search (Real-Time Availability)

    Start with real-time domain engines rather than manual single-lookup tools.

    • Use tools like Instant Domain Search or Namecheap to check .com, .tv, and .media concurrently as you type.
    • Instantly identifies if the primary domain is available, registered, parked, or premium.
  2. 2. ICANN / WHOIS Lookup (If .com is Taken)

    If the preferred .com is registered, query official registry records to evaluate acquisition potential.

    • Perform a query at lookup.icann.org.
    • Check the domain registrant, creation date, expiration date, and managing registrar.
    • Determine whether it belongs to an active operating business or a domain broker/parking site.
  3. 3. Federal Trademark Search (USPTO TESS Database)

    Verify that the proposed name does not infringe on registered broadcast or entertainment trademarks.

    • Access the USPTO Trademark Search database at uspto.gov.
    • Focus specifically on Class 038 (Telecommunications & Broadcasting) and Class 041 (Education, Entertainment & Media Production).
    • Ensure no active media or broadcast entity holds a conflicting mark in those specific classes.
  4. 4. Social Media Handle Availability

    Check cross-platform username availability across video and social media channels.

    • Use aggregate checkers like Namechk to inspect YouTube, X, Rumble, Telegram, and Instagram simultaneously.
    • If exact handles are taken, evaluate standardized modifiers such as @SourceStationTV or @SourceFieldMedia.
  5. 5. SEO Footprint & Keyword Suppression Analysis

    Execute exact-match search engine queries to ensure the name can rank organically.

    • Search exact phrase variations in quotes (e.g., "Source Station" or "Source Field") across Google and Brave.
    • Confirm the name isn't already dominated by government entities, major media conglomerates, or suppression algorithms.
