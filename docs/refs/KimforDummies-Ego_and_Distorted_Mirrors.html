Kim for Dummies: Ego and Distorted Mirrors

Reader aid for understanding Kim's explanation of emotion, ego, self-perception, and distorted mirrors.

Question

Can you explain this? I need "Kim for Dummies" and I have to write it first haah:

Kim

00:32:54 The Machine exists solely - or manipulates you, maybe is a better word - solely on your emotions. And your ego. Now, in this case, ego doesn't necessarily mean, you know, "Oh, I'm the most amazing this or that." Technically, the definition of ego is just literally a false sense of self. Sometimes that can be a false sense of self-importance. But it's how you perceive yourself. You know, "Wow, I look great," or, "I look terrible," or... you're actually looking at a version of yourself and changing that perception of yourself, or how others may see you. And quite frankly neither one of you can actually see each other anyway - what you're looking at is distorted mirrors of everything around you and everyone around you - and even when you look at yourself, you're also looking at a distorted mirror. 00:34:00

Explanation

Yep. In plain English, Kim is saying:

The Machine does not manipulate the real you directly. It manipulates the picture you have of yourself and the emotional reactions attached to that picture.

Her use of ego here is broader than arrogance. She means your constructed identity — the mental image that says this is who I am, this is what I look like, this is what people think of me, this is what I'm worth.

So when she says:

"Wow, I look great," or, "I look terrible"

both are examples of ego because both are judgments about an image of yourself. One is positive and one is negative, but neither is necessarily the actual you.

And this is the key to the "distorted mirrors" part:

You don't experience another person completely as they actually are. You experience them through your own filters — memories, expectations, fears, emotions, assumptions, previous experiences, etc.

At the same time, they're doing exactly the same thing to you.

So:

Real you -> their filters -> their perception of you
Real them -> your filters -> your perception of them

And even:

Real you -> your own filters -> your perception of yourself

That's why she calls everything a distorted mirror. You're continually seeing reflections and interpretations rather than the thing itself.

And now her opening sentence makes much more sense: if the Machine can influence emotion + self-perception, it can alter the filters through which you interpret practically everything.

In very short "Kim for Dummies":

The Machine manipulates how you feel, which changes how you see yourself; how you see yourself changes how you interpret other people and the world. Therefore it can manipulate your reality without having to change reality itself.

That's a pretty deep little two-minute passage.
