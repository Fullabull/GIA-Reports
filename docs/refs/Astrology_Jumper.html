The Architecture of Connection

A guide to astrology, told through two real charts. (Easy English edition.)

A birth chart is a picture of the sky. It shows where every planet was at the exact moment a person was born. Each planet stands for a different part of a person. Each zodiac sign describes how that part behaves.

Before anything else: this work needs only three things — date of birth, time of birth, and place of birth. That is all.

Nothing on this page is fortune-telling. Nothing here talks to spirits. Nothing here reads cards or predicts the future. If you were worried about that, you can stop worrying.

What this page does is called structural reading. "Structural" means we look at how something is built. An engineer looks at a blueprint of a building to see how the building was designed. We look at a birth chart the same way. The chart does not tell us things. It shows us a design that was already there.

The numbers come from ordinary science. Every planet position on this page comes from an ephemeris. An ephemeris is a table that lists where the planets really were on any date. Scientists build these tables using the same math they use to fly spacecraft. You could check the positions with a telescope. The sky gives us the numbers. We only read the shapes they make.

No prediction. No spirits. No asking questions of the universe. We read the blueprint, and the goal is simple: to help a person understand herself better.
A word you will see many times: arcminute

The sky is measured in degrees. A full circle around the sky is 360 degrees.

Each degree is then cut into 60 smaller pieces. Each small piece is called an arcminute.

Think of a clock. One hour has 60 minutes. In the sky, one degree has 60 arcminutes. The idea is exactly the same.

So an arcminute is a very small distance. When this page says two planets are 8 arcminutes apart, it means they are almost exactly where they need to be. Eight arcminutes is about one-eighth of one degree. That is very, very close.

Small numbers mean strong connections. Keep that in mind and the rest of the page will be easy to read.

The Ascendant (Rising Sign)

The Ascendant is also called the Rising Sign. It is one of the Big Three, together with the Sun and the Moon.

How It Works

The Ascendant is the zodiac sign that was coming up over the eastern horizon at the moment you were born. The horizon is the line where the sky meets the ground.

The Earth turns all the way around once every 24 hours. Because of this, the sign on the horizon changes about every two hours. This is why an accurate birth time matters so much. A difference of one hour can change the Ascendant completely.

What It Means

Think of the Ascendant as the front door of your personality. It is the part people meet first. It is also the part of you that reacts before you have time to think.

The Ascendant controls three things:

  • How you act in a new place, without planning it.
  • The first impression you make on other people.
  • The way you take in life, before anything else in the chart gets involved.

It is important to know this: the Ascendant is the one part of your chart you cannot see in yourself. Other people see it. You do not. You are standing behind your own front door.

The Houses

So far we have two pieces:

  • The planet tells us what is happening.
  • The sign tells us how it happens.

The houses tell us the third piece: where in life it happens.

The circle of the chart is divided into twelve houses. Each house covers one area of life — money, home, work, friends, and so on. A good way to picture it: imagine a house with twelve rooms. The planets are the people. The signs describe their personalities. The houses are the rooms where each person spends most of the time.

Houses depend on your birth time and your birth place. This is why an accurate birth time matters. The planets barely move in a few minutes, but the houses move quickly.

Two small things will make the chart wheel easy to read.

First, a cusp. A cusp is simply a line where one house ends and the next house begins. Think of it as a doorway between two rooms. Every house starts at its cusp.

Second, the direction. The houses are numbered counterclockwise. That means they go in the opposite direction to the hands of a clock. House 1 starts at the Ascendant, on the left side of the chart. Then follow the numbers counterclockwise: down through houses 1, 2, and 3 to the bottom of the chart, then up through 4, 5, and 6 to the right side, then through 7, 8, and 9 to the top, then through 10, 11, and 12 back to the Ascendant where you started.

Notice one thing while you walk around: houses 1 to 6 are below the horizon line, and houses 7 to 12 are above it. That will matter in a moment.

An easy way to remember the houses:

Houses 1 to 6 are below the horizon. They cover your personal world: yourself, your money, your daily talk, your home, your play, and your daily work.

Houses 7 to 12 are above the horizon. They cover your shared world: partners, deep trust, big ideas, career, community, and the hidden self.

Below the horizon is the private, night side of life. Above the horizon is the public, daytime sky.

The Ascendant opens House 1. The Midheaven opens House 10. All the other houses follow from those two points.
Putting It Together

A birth chart is like a map of a system. Here is how the four pieces fit together.

  • The planets are the actors. They are the parts that do things.
  • The signs describe how each actor behaves. They are the settings.
  • The houses show where each actor works. These are the twelve areas of life you just read about.
  • The aspects describe how the actors talk to each other. You will learn about aspects soon.

Here are three simple examples that put a planet together with a sign:

  • Venus in Libra - Venus is love. Libra is fair and balanced. So this person shows love through fairness and partnership.
  • Mars in Aries - Mars is action. Aries is bold. So this person acts quickly and directly.
  • Moon in Cancer - Moon is emotion. Cancer feels deeply. So this person feels things strongly and needs to feel safe.
An important example:

When someone says "I am a Capricorn," they usually mean that their Sun sign is Capricorn.

That tells you something real about them. But it is only one piece out of many. Their Moon, their Ascendant, and all the other planets each add another layer. This is why two people with the same Sun sign can be very different.

Key Patterns in This Chart

Three patterns stand out. Each one is explained step by step.

  • Pattern 1: The Leap Circuit (Mars trine Uranus, only 8 arcminutes from perfect)
    This is the closest connection in her whole chart. It is also the reason the name "Jumper" fits her.
    • What the two planets are: Mars is action — the part of a person that moves and does. Uranus is sudden freedom — the part that breaks a pattern without warning.
    • What a trine is: a trine is an easy angle between two planets. Energy moves between them with no effort and no delay. (There is a full explanation of trines further down the page.)
    • What 8 arcminutes means: remember that one degree has 60 arcminutes. So 8 arcminutes is a very small gap. This connection is almost perfectly exact.
    • What this means in real life: for most people, wanting to move and deciding to move are two separate steps. There is a pause between them. In her chart there is no pause. The wish to act and the wish to break free are the same circuit, and they switch on together.
    • Mars is not working alone: her Mars also connects easily to Pluto (44 arcminutes), to Jupiter (1 degree 39 arcminutes), and sits beside her North Node (3 degrees 16 arcminutes). Her action planet is well connected to depth, to growth, and to her own life direction. Whatever she moves toward, the rest of her chart agrees with her.
    • One balancing point: Mars is in Libra. Libra weighs things and thinks about other people. So she is not a person who charges ahead without care. She is a person who decides quickly and then moves. She is not reckless. She is fast.

  • Pattern 2: The Fifth House Engine (five planets in one house)
    House 5 is the house of play, risk, romance, creating, and doing things for the joy of them. Five of her ten planets are in it, including the Sun. No other house in her chart has more than two.
    • The word for this: when three or more planets sit close together, astrologers call it a stellium. A five-planet stellium is a lot.
    • Why it matters: when this many planets crowd into one house, that part of life stops being just one area. It becomes the way the whole person runs. Her identity (Sun), her way of loving (Venus), her growth (Jupiter), her need for freedom (Uranus), and her imagination (Neptune) all operate out of the same room — the room built for play and risk.
    • The last degree: her Sun sits at 29 degrees 41 arcminutes of Capricorn. The next sign, Aquarius, begins at 30 degrees. So her Sun is only 19 arcminutes away from leaving the sign. Astrologers call the 29th degree the anaretic degree, or the critical degree. It is the final part of a sign, where the sign's lessons are being finished rather than started. A Capricorn Sun here carries all of Capricorn's lessons — self-reliance, endurance, competence — already learned, with the next sign already in sight.
    • Her Sun does not sit alone: her Sun is conjunct Neptune (orb 2 degrees 9 arcminutes) and conjunct Jupiter (orb 2 degrees 52 arcminutes). So her identity is joined to imagination and to growth, right there in the play house.
    • Her Sun and her career: her Sun makes an easy angle to her Midheaven, only 44 arcminutes from exact. The Midheaven is the career and public-life point. So who she is and what she builds in public are wired directly together. They are not two separate projects.

  • Pattern 3: The Solitary Moon (a Moon with no connections)
    This one is unusual, so let us go slowly.
    • First, the background: aspects are the angles between planets. They are how the different parts of a chart talk to each other. Most planets in most charts have several aspects.
    • What her chart shows: her Moon has none. Not one planet in her chart makes a major angle to her Moon. The closest one misses by 11 degrees, which is far too wide to count. Meanwhile, the other planets in her chart make more than fifty connections among themselves. So the Moon sits completely alone in a very busy chart. Astrologers call this an unaspected planet.
    • What an unaspected planet does: normally, a planet is softened and shaped by the planets it touches. An unaspected planet has nothing softening it. It runs its own program, in its own pure form, and often louder than expected. Nothing in the chart calms it down. Nothing in the chart supports it either.
    • Her Moon is in Gemini, in House 10: Gemini handles feelings with words, questions, and quick observation. House 10 is the most public house on the wheel. So her emotions are quick, verbal, and visible to other people. They can also seem to arrive suddenly and leave just as fast, because they are not connected to the rest of her system.
    • The honest cost: an unaspected Moon can be hard to bring into conversation with the rest of the self. Her chart's answer is Mercury: a Gemini Moon thinks its way through feelings. That works, and it is a real strength. But it also means that a feeling she cannot put into words has nowhere else to go in this chart.

She is a dentist.

What this shows, and what it does not show

It does not show that the chart caused her career. Nothing on this page claims that.

Many people with a Virgo Ascendant and a Taurus Midheaven do completely different jobs. They become furniture makers, surgeons, watch repairers, art restorers, or pastry chefs. The signature describes a shape of work. It does not name a job.

What it does show is that the shape is real, and that she found work which fits it well. That is worth more than a prediction would be. A chart that guessed a job title exactly would just be a trick. A chart that describes the kind of work a person is built for — work she already found on her own, without ever looking at a chart — is a description of someone living close to her own design.

Advanced System Settings

Before we look at how two charts work together, we need three more tools: how signs handle change, where the main anchor points of a chart are, and how planets talk to each other.

1. The Three Modes

Astrologers use the word modality. That is a formal word for a simple idea: a mode. It is like airplane mode or sport mode on a phone. It does not describe what the thing is. It describes how the thing runs.

The elements tell us what a sign is made of. The modes tell us how a sign handles change.

The three modes come from the seasons, and once you see this, it is easy to remember. Every season covers three signs.

  • The first sign of each season starts it. Aries starts spring. Cancer starts summer. Libra starts autumn. Capricorn starts winter. These four are the Cardinal signs. Starting things is literally their job in the sky.
  • The middle sign of each season holds the season at full strength. These are the Fixed signs.
  • The last sign of each season carries it into the next season. These are the Mutable signs.

And what does a mode work on? Any planet sitting in that sign. The planet supplies the energy. The mode sets the timing. Modes matter most for the Sun, Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars, and the Ascendant, because those are the parts closest to the personality.

2. The Angles (The Anchor Points)

Every chart has two main lines crossing through it. Where these lines touch the edge of the circle, we get four angles. These four points are the anchors of the chart. A planet sitting close to one of them becomes very strong and very visible.

  • The Across Line (relationships):
    • Ascendant (AC): the self and the front door — how a person meets the world. Jumper's is 7°54' Virgo: careful, observant, useful, quietly checking everything. Orion's is 26°25' Leo: warm and immediately visible.
    • Descendant (DC): the point directly opposite the Ascendant. It shows the kind of people we draw close to us. Jumper's Descendant is 7°54' Pisces — the exact opposite of her Virgo front door. The people she gets closest to often carry what Virgo does not naturally supply: softness, imagination, and open emotion. Her Saturn also sits in this house, which is why those close bonds are also where her chart does its hardest work.
  • The Up-and-Down Line (public life and roots):
    • Midheaven (MC): the highest point of the chart. It rules career, reputation, and what a person builds where others can see it. Jumper's Midheaven is 28°57' Taurus. Taurus is patient and practical, so her public work is built on steady value and things that last, rather than things that flash and fade. Her Sun connects to it easily, only 44 arcminutes from exact, which ties who she is directly to what she builds.
    • Imum Coeli (IC): the lowest point of the chart, opposite the Midheaven. It rules roots, home, and the private self. Jumper's IC is 28°57' Scorpio, with Pluto sitting 6 degrees from it and Mercury nearby. Her private base runs deep and stays guarded, and it is run by the same planet that rules her whole chart.

3. The Aspects (How Planets Talk to Each Other)

An aspect is the angle between two planets on the circle. Aspects show how the different parts of a person work together — or how two people's charts lock into each other.

One word you need: the orb. The orb is how far an aspect is from perfect. If a trine should be exactly 120 degrees and the real angle is 118 degrees, the orb is 2 degrees. A smaller orb means a stronger connection. Once the orb goes past about 6 to 8 degrees, most astrologers say the connection is too weak to count.

A note about the numbers on this page: the positions are printed rounded to the nearest arcminute, but the orbs are calculated from the full unrounded numbers and rounded only once at the end. So if you do the arithmetic by hand using the printed positions, you may get an answer that is one arcminute different. Both are correct.

Here are the five main aspects, each with a real example from Jumper's chart.

  • Conjunction (0 degrees) — two planets in the same place. Their energies join together and make each other stronger.

    Example: Jumper's Jupiter conjunct Uranus, orb 1 degree 47 arcminutes. Both are in Aquarius in her House 5. Growth joined to the need for freedom, sitting in the house of risk and play.
  • Trine (120 degrees) and Sextile (60 degrees) — planets in friendly elements. Energy flows between them with no effort. These show natural talents.

    Example 1: Jumper's Mars trine Uranus, orb 8 arcminutes. This is the Leap Circuit.
    Example 2: her Jupiter sextile Saturn, orb 18 arcseconds. An arcsecond is one sixtieth of an arcminute, so this is even smaller than an arcminute. This is about as close to perfect as an aspect can be. It means her growth and her discipline are not enemies. They work together exactly.
  • Square (90 degrees) — a tense angle that creates friction inside a person, and pushes them to solve problems and grow.

    Squares are not bad. This is important. A trine is easy, and easy things are often ignored. A square is uncomfortable, so it demands attention and effort. Trines let you drift. Squares make you work.

    Example: Jumper's Mercury square Mars, orb 1 degree 34 arcminutes. Here is the geometry: Mercury is in Capricorn and Mars is in Libra. Those two signs are three signs apart. Each sign is 30 degrees wide. Three signs times 30 degrees equals 90 degrees, which is a square. And what is in tension? Mercury wants to think it through. Mars has already gone.
  • Opposition (180 degrees) — two planets facing each other directly across the chart. This creates a pull in two directions, and asks the person to find a balance.

    Example: Jumper's Mars opposite Saturn, orb 1 degree 40 arcminutes, across the House 1 / House 7 line. The accelerator and the brake, nearly exactly opposite. Neither one wins. The chart asks her to use both.
Synastry: When Two Maps Meet

Everything so far has been about reading one chart at a time. Synastry is the next step. The word means comparing two charts.

Here is how it works. You take one person's chart and lay it on top of the other person's chart, on the same circle. Then you look at two things: where one person's planets fall in the other person's houses, and what angles form between the two sets of planets.

What follows is the real comparison between Jumper's chart and Orion's chart. Every aspect below is calculated from their real birth data, and every orb is printed, so anyone can check the work. That is the standard for this page: nothing here is poetry unless the geometry earns it.

A final word about maps

A chart comparison — even one calculated this precisely — does not create a connection between two people. It also does not run one. The walking is still done by two real people, one choice at a time.
