UNN Platform Strategy: Kajabi vs GoHighLevel

Focused comparison - September 8, 2026

Executive flyover

Both platforms are credible contenders, but they solve the problem from different directions.

Kajabi

Best shorthand: polished media / membership / community business platform.

Its advantage is cohesion and administrative simplicity. It is easier to imagine a non-technical UNN team operating it day to day without constantly thinking about the machinery underneath.

GoHighLevel

Best shorthand: programmable CRM / automation / membership / community operating system.

Its advantage is adaptability. HighLevel is unusually good at changing who gets access to what, when, and why - without rebuilding the platform around a new business rule.

The strategic question is not simply which has more features. It is whether UNN values Kajabi's polish and simplicity more, or HighLevel's configurability and architectural headroom. Either can work; the difference becomes more important as UNN changes its membership model over time.

Architect for change - whichever platform wins

The safest architecture assumes that Kajabi or HighLevel may someday be replaced. That does not mean avoiding SaaS. It means avoiding unnecessary dependency on any one SaaS implementation.

UNN should keep the important concepts independent of the platform:

  • Members are UNN members, not "Kajabi contacts" or "HighLevel contacts."
  • Membership status is UNN business data, not merely a permission switch buried inside a page builder.
  • Media masters belong in an independent UNN-controlled archive. The platform copy is the delivery copy, not the only copy.
  • Payment-provider IDs, subscription IDs and billing dates must be preserved.
  • APIs and webhooks should be preferred for important integrations so UNN can replace one component without redesigning the entire organization.
  • Business rules should be documented outside the platform. For example: "Trial members get Premium for 30 days, then fall to Free unless subscribed." That rule should exist as a written UNN rule, not only as twelve unexplained automation boxes.

The goal: change the platform without changing the business - and change the business without changing the platform.

API strength: both are capable, but HighLevel has more architectural headroom

Kajabi API

Kajabi's current Public API is substantially better than old descriptions of Kajabi suggest. It can work with contacts and their Offers, including granting and revoking Offer access programmatically. Kajabi also supports webhooks.

This makes Kajabi quite capable of participating in an external UNN architecture rather than being a sealed box.

Assessment: strong enough to take seriously.

HighLevel API

HighLevel exposes a broader operating surface: contacts, messaging, workflows, calendars, payments, subscriptions, custom payment providers and webhooks are all part of its developer ecosystem.

This matters if UNN eventually wants its own member portal, external search, specialized publishing tools, independent reporting, or a new front end while retaining some HighLevel services behind it.

Assessment: the stronger platform for "architect for change."

HighLevel's unusual agility: grant / revoke instead of rebuild

This may be HighLevel's most strategically interesting characteristic for UNN.

HighLevel workflows can grant and revoke Community group access. They can also grant or revoke access to a specific private channel while leaving the person inside the larger Community group.

New UNN member -> join Free community -> grant Premium access -> wait 30 days -> IF subscribed: keep Premium -> ELSE: revoke Premium -> member remains in Free community

That same pattern can be rearranged without rebuilding UNN:

30-day full trial 7-day delayed Kim access Free / Supporter / Premium Read-only channels Temporary event access Founding member access Grace period after payment failure

This is why HighLevel deserves special attention even if Kajabi looks cleaner in a demo. HighLevel treats access as something the system can continuously change in response to events. That is unusually compatible with a UNN business model that may evolve.

The crown jewels are not the website - they are the member and subscription relationships

A platform migration can survive redesigned pages. It becomes dangerous when UNN loses track of who the customer is, what they bought, whether they are active, and when they should next be billed.

UNN should be able to reproduce, outside the platform, at minimum:

  • UNN member ID / internal identifier
  • name and email
  • membership/tier
  • status: free, trialing, active, past due, canceled, etc.
  • trial start/end dates
  • subscription start date
  • next billing date
  • price / billing interval / currency
  • payment provider
  • provider customer ID
  • provider subscription ID
  • cancellation date / reason where available
  • entitlements: what that membership is supposed to unlock

Never treat a CSV export as a comforting checkbox. Perform a test export, import it into a blank system or database, and prove that the fields needed to reconstruct membership are actually there.

Payments: preserve portability deliberately

Where practical, UNN should prefer a payment architecture in which the recurring customer relationship is held by a portable payment processor such as UNN's own Stripe account, rather than becoming inseparable from the application vendor.

Kajabi

Kajabi can work with Stripe and also offers Kajabi Payments. Kajabi documents CSV export of subscriptions and continued management of customer payments after account cancellation.

However, before moving active subscriptions into Kajabi Payments, UNN should demand a written answer describing the reverse migration path if UNN later leaves Kajabi. Kajabi clearly documents migration into Kajabi Payments from Stripe; the exit direction deserves equal scrutiny.

HighLevel

HighLevel can connect a Stripe account directly and exposes subscription information through both CSV export and API. Its developer platform can also support custom payment-provider integrations.

That does not make migration automatic, but it gives UNN more options for keeping payment processing conceptually separate from the Community/front-end platform.

Plan the exit before signing the entrance

Ownership is not the same as portability. A vendor may truthfully say, "You own your content," while still leaving UNN with months of reconstruction work when it departs.

Before signing either platform, ask the vendor to demonstrate - in writing and preferably in a test account - exactly what UNN receives on exit:

  • full contact/member export
  • subscription and transaction export
  • payment-provider/customer/subscription IDs
  • member profiles and custom fields
  • course/product/access assignments
  • media and attachments
  • Community posts and comments
  • chat/direct-message history, if legally/exportably available
  • channels/groups and membership relationships
  • automation/workflow definitions
  • analytics/history
  • API access during the migration period

Current concern for both: neither platform should receive a green check for "entire Community export" until it actually demonstrates a usable bulk export/API path for the social history UNN cares about.

A sane exit-management policy from Day One

1. Keep independent masters of all important UNN media/content. 2. Export contacts + subscriptions + transactions on a regular schedule. 3. Preserve external payment-provider identifiers. 4. Document membership/access rules outside the vendor platform. 5. Use APIs/webhooks for important integrations where practical. 6. Periodically test that exported data can actually be reconstructed. 7. Before any future move, build the replacement in parallel. 8. Migrate copies, test logins/access/billing, then cut over. 9. Keep the old platform alive through verification. 10. Cancel the old platform last.

The old system should never be dismantled first and then everybody crosses their fingers.

Provisional conclusion

Kajabi argument

If the priority is getting a polished UNN membership/media/community operation running reliably with a manageable staff learning curve, Kajabi has the cleaner story.

HighLevel argument

If the priority is building UNN so access models, workflows, integrations and even pieces of the architecture can change repeatedly without another platform migration, HighLevel has the more interesting long-term story.

Do not choose yet on feature count. Make both vendors demonstrate the same UNN scenarios: no-card 30-day trial, trial-to-free fallback, delayed premium access, payment failure/grace period, tier upgrade/downgrade, bulk export, and a hypothetical migration away. The platform that can demonstrate those cleanly is telling UNN much more than a sales presentation can.
