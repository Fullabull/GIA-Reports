Summary

Kim launches Declassified -- Beyond the Headlines with Episode One, recorded under time pressure and shipped audio-only rather than held back. The first half is housekeeping on the United Network rebuild. The team has been studying "algo speak" and found that the word "news" -- in a title, a logo, or a brand -- gets a channel buried, and that a shadow ban is permanent even if the offending content is deleted. UNN will therefore start fresh across YouTube, Spotify, Apple Podcasts, Instagram and Facebook, and Kim asks the community to help rename the network. New categories are rolling out: World Watch for regional stories, The Current for interviews and binge playlists, Life Redefined replacing the old New Earth series, and Shorts to Share for material aimed at friends and family who are not ready for simulation talk. Field Messenger reports now run under The REEL, and The Pulse replaces the single long weekly broadcast.

The intelligence portion opens on the collapse of the mainstream narrative. A New York Times insider reports the paper is redacting constantly because the material coming from the narrative machine -- operatives inside the White House, MI6, Langley, and services in Iran, Russia and Ukraine -- keeps proving false. Kim reminds listeners that mainstream news is written two days ahead, a fact given to her by a former Viacom executive-branch source, so a planned event that fails to occur forces a same-day rewrite of the entire rotation. The Times began printing source disclaimers on individual articles, then quietly moved the language into a rewritten code of ethics that nobody reads. Kim adopts a community member's term for the result: churnalism. She reads the Nepal glacier-collapse coverage the same way -- flood footage that people on the ground do not recognize, AI-generated "man on the street" segments with six-fingered interviewees, and a Deep State artifact hunt as the actual story behind the event.

Jared Kushner occupies the middle of the report. Iran is now pursuing him over a $9 billion insider-trading incident, and he was caught building a Jeffrey Epstein-style resort in Albania -- financed not by the Iranian money but by a Turkish cocaine dealer, on Ottoman-era documents forged to seize land from its current owners. Kim ties the choice of Albania to the Universal Trust, known in the black market as the Albanian Trust: roughly 280 years old, once holding hundreds of trillions, a laundromat charging 35% that served every cartel and mafia on Earth, with a back door into central banks worldwide. Its second business was holding dirty money aside to fund political campaigns, then using that funding as leverage on the presidents it bought -- as it did to Colombia's president within the last year. Kushner nevertheless remained on a White House financial call last night alongside Treasury and Federal Reserve members.

The oil and money section follows from there. Iran has not legally sold oil since 1995, but it has never stopped selling it -- across the Pakistani border on dirt bikes at a dollar a barrel through the CPEC corridor into China, through Kuwait, through Africa, and into Saudi Aramco, which Kim identifies as the oil laundering company for the entire Middle East, relabeling sanctioned crude as its own production. Big oil pays $25 a barrel or less while the Strait of Hormuz story drives pump prices up, and Venezuelan oil now flows into U.S. refineries nearly free. Behind it, the Treasury Department has been running on fake money from AI screens supplied by the operatives of Durango, convincing banks worldwide to buy treasuries that are not backed by anything. That is now failing, and Kim reports full-on panic ahead of the U.S. market open. The Federal Reserve, the Bank for International Settlements, the World Bank and the IMF have all become non-player characters, and the plan is to announce a global Treasury as the Fed's righteous replacement.

Kim closes on the data centers going up worldwide at extraordinary speed, funded through Universal Trust access and built with 3D-printing technology far beyond consumer machines. Reports around them describe dying cattle, wildlife and plants, contaminated or vanishing water, an electromagnetic field unpleasant to stand near, and a constant hum carrying 50 to 100 miles. Something underneath these sites is different from the Microsoft, Honeywell and NSA ECHELON facilities that have existed for decades. She advises EMP shielding and an alternative water supply for anyone living nearby, asks Field Messengers to report on the fields and the hum, and sends the detail to this week's Beyond the Matrix as too much for Declassified.
