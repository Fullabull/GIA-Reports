Summary

Kim opens the October 30, 2026 report, with Tank, by bringing the series back under the GIA name and starting a new topic: what you really own in a debt system -- your labor, your home, your land, and the infrastructure around you. An asset is anything that produces value for someone, and because you can work and produce, you are an asset. Long ago, governments ran more like an employee-owned company: citizens held real shares in the land, such as mineral rights, and what people produced came back to everyone. Today that value lands on someone else's balance sheet instead. In a Natural world it is simple -- you do something and you earn something for it -- and that is the difference between real currency and the debt notes we are paid in now. Because we are paid in debt notes, people work mostly for free: after taxes and purchases they keep perhaps 10-15% of what they make, and even the cash in your pocket is debt you still owe back to the system. Products are built to wear out so you keep buying, which keeps everyone "living in lack," and disease keeps the sick and their families paying into the medical system, because nothing is cured -- it only goes into remission.

Kim then walks through the bond machinery. Banks pool mortgages in the MERS system, grade them by risk, and put a bond on top that is sold to the Fed or to private firms such as BlackRock -- so the bondholder, not the homeowner, owns the deed. Privatization, under way since 1975, put government services such as Child Protective Services in private hands, and every lender, even your employer, owns a piece of your birth certificate bond. All of these bonds end up at ANNA, the Belgium-based numbering agency, where new bonds are created in the name of preserving life -- the job of the Primordials, the first-created beings. That ended in 2007, and the markets crashed in 2008. Broke governments now squeeze bonds out of traffic tickets, land fines, privatized prisons, and indictments.

Beneath it all are the prisoner bonds. Humanity was captured by the Machine, whose bloodline is the fallen Primordials, and is held in the Matrix: a debtors' prison enforced by the International Court of Justice and the Elite's private version of it, whose staff are the prison guards and "serve the Nephilim." Everything is taken at imprisonment -- that is, at birth -- and because the debt can never be paid, people come back to keep paying; that is incarnation. The same hidden terms in birth certificates and mortgages have let wars and bombings swap people out for cloned, soulless replacements (not the original people). Without new prisoner bonds, the treasuries, the Fed, and BlackRock cannot issue bonds, and there is no time jump left to reset the system.

Governments are now in emergency meetings, with the Treasury trying to tap the ANNA prisoner bonds and the crypto markets to prop up stablecoin. Last weekend was the "time of assessment": every Primordial went back to Source and failed, losing the authority to create, and Source has made other arrangements for Creation. Deep State operatives who gathered early under New York had been waiting to be reassigned as the Primordials' Nephilim. The loss strips authority from the Machine as well, and one declaration ending the prisoner bonds would crash the market. They have lost, and now they want help from you.

Kim closes by asking who wants another billion years in debtors' prison, or to support the Deep State, the ICC, the International Court of Justice, or the World Health Organization: "I wonder who's in debt now?" More comes in the next report, and the GIA is back as a weekly series.
